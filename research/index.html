Research

I build perception systems for robots. For the last four years that has meant making one camera do the work the field assumed needed a depth sensor.

Prologue

The same question was asked at Peradeniya in 2017: an autonomous exploration planning strategy for a robot mapping an unknown space, using a laser range finder fused with stereo cameras. It is being asked again 10 years later with a learned model and a single camera. That paper is still on IEEE Xplore and the project video is still online.

Part I. Structuring the seen

Building metric-semantic hierarchical 3D scene graphs from a single camera and an IMU, in real time.

Metric-semantic mapping systems that produce usable scene graphs have almost always assumed RGB-D or LiDAR input, because reliable geometry is the hard part and a depth sensor supplies it directly. That assumption rules out the platforms where the mapping is most useful, since payload and power are exactly what limit a small drone. Removing the depth sensor was the question, and four papers answer it in sequence.

Mono-Hydra, 2023. Proving it is possible

Learned depth and learned semantics, running as two separate networks, feed a robocentric square-root visual-inertial odometry front end, and the resulting semantic mesh drives the layered scene graph. Measured against a LiDAR backpack point cloud of the ITC building it reached 0.19 m and 0.21 m mean error on two floors, at 15 fps. Read more.

M2H, 2025. One model instead of two

Two independent networks duplicate compute and produce two representations that do not agree with each other. M2H collapses them into a single multi-task model in which depth, semantics, surface normals, and edges exchange information through window-based cross-task attention. The benchmark numbers improved, and so did the map: 0.11 m on the same building, at twice the frame rate. Read more.

M2H-MX, 2026. Testing whether the map actually cares

A better benchmark score is not the same thing as a better map, and dense prediction papers usually stop before finding out. M2H-MX is evaluated twice, once as a predictor and once as the front end inside an otherwise unchanged SLAM pipeline. Average trajectory error on selected ScanNet sequences fell from 17.59 cm to 6.91 cm, which puts a monocular system close to the RGB-D baselines in the same table. The ablation attributes most of the gain to the backbone features rather than to the new decoder blocks. Read more.

Mono-Hydra++, under review. Closing the loop backwards

Everything so far runs perception forwards into mapping. Mono-Hydra++ sends it back the other way as well: predicted sparse depth enters the estimator as metric anchor factors, semantic masks keep dynamic regions out of it, and the poses that come back out temporally align the next dense predictions. 0.08 m on the ITC second floor, 0.033 m calibrated trajectory error on 7-Scenes. Read more.

Part II. Exploring the unseen In progress

A reinforcement learning agent with a learned world model, choosing where to look next in a building it has never seen.

Status

The formulation, the world model, the objective, and the training setup are written up in the thesis. The experiments are not run yet, so there are no results to report and none are claimed here. The exploration agent also does not consume the Mono-Hydra++ scene graph: it maintains its own topological memory over an occupancy map built from simulated depth and pose. Connecting the two parts is the next step.

The robot enters an unmapped indoor space with one forward-facing camera and has to cover it under a time budget without collision. Motion is treated as solved: a geometric planner proposes only routes it has verified against the occupancy map the robot has built. The open problem is choice, not motion, so the learned layer decides among certified options and may abstain, but never commands motion itself.

Predict consequences, not appearances.

The learned component sits between two deterministic layers and has no path to the actuators except by selecting an option the planner above it has already certified as safe. It may re-order those options and it may abstain. It may not invent one. Three requirements follow from the shape of the task rather than from the hardware:

  • Options are judged against one another, so two routes leading through the same opening are not treated as genuine alternatives.
  • Actions are temporally extended, because passing through a doorway is locally expensive and locally uninformative, and everything of value happens afterwards.
  • Branch memory gives the agent a persistent notion of unfinished work, so regions get completed and exhausted ones get abandoned on evidence rather than on a timer.
  • No learned component is permitted to command motion. It re-orders certified options or refuses them.
