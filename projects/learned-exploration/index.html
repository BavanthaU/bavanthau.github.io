Projects · Part II

Learned exploration

A reinforcement learning agent with a learned world model that predicts the consequences of each route it could take, and picks one.

2025 to present · Work in progress, no results yet

Status

The formulation, the world model, the objective, and the training setup are written up in the thesis. The experiments are not run yet, so there are no results to report and none are claimed here. The exploration agent also does not consume the Mono-Hydra++ scene graph: it maintains its own topological memory over an occupancy map built from simulated depth and pose. Connecting the two parts is the next step.

What it does

The robot enters an unmapped indoor space with one forward-facing camera and has to cover it under a time budget without collision. Motion is treated as solved: a geometric planner proposes only routes it has verified against the occupancy map the robot has built. The open problem is choice, not motion, so the learned layer decides among certified options and may abstain, but never commands motion itself.

How the agent decides

A world model that predicts consequences, not pixels

For each candidate route the model predicts the seven quantities the decision uses: new area mapped, elapsed time, distance travelled, whether the robot enters a room for the first time, how the structural picture changes in entities and open frontiers, whether the route fails through abort or timeout or safety refusal, and whether the region stays worth working on afterwards. The model has no appearance term and none of its outputs is a pixel.

A compressed next state, for looking further ahead

Alongside the consequence vector the model predicts the structural picture that would follow execution, which allows predictions to be chained into a multi-step lookahead. Whether the lookahead is worth its cost is untested, and is one of the questions for the experiments.

Telling randomness apart from ignorance

Running the same route twice from the same state gives different results, and that spread belongs to the world. Not having seen anything comparable is a different problem and belongs to the model. An ensemble of models trained on different slices of experience separates the two: the mean predicted variance is the world's, the variance of the predicted means is the model's. When the second one is high the agent filters options and hands control back to the deterministic layer.

Supervision from same-state counterfactuals

A robot takes one action per decision, so logged experience never records what the roads not taken would have produced, and recovering them from other episodes is confounded by different histories. In simulation the entire world state is captured at a decision point, physics and map and controller internals and random number streams included, one option is run to completion, the world is restored, and the next option is run. Every alternative is then measured from an identical starting point, so the differences between outcomes are attributable to the choice alone.

Rewarding the outcome rather than its proxies

New roadmap nodes and distance covered are cheap to measure but uninformative: adding detail inside an already-mapped room scores well, while crossing a doorway is expensive and scores nothing immediately. The leading reward term is new area per second of flight, because time rather than distance is the binding resource. First entries into rooms and portals carry one-off rewards because they change what the robot can reach.

Design commitments

Certification creates, learning selects.

  • Options are judged against one another, so two routes leading through the same opening are not treated as genuine alternatives.
  • Actions are temporally extended, because passing through a doorway is locally expensive and locally uninformative, and everything of value happens afterwards.
  • Branch memory gives the agent a persistent notion of unfinished work, so regions get completed and exhausted ones get abandoned on evidence rather than on a timer.
  • No learned component is permitted to command motion. It re-orders certified options or refuses them.

What is being tested

If exploration is represented as topological branches and temporally extended programs, if the consequences of those programs are predicted rather than their appearances, and if supervision comes from exact same-state counterfactuals rather than logged trajectories, then the agent should finish the regions it starts, leave them when the evidence says they are exhausted rather than when time runs out, and become conservative where it is ignorant.
