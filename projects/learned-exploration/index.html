ATLAS

Topology-aware learned attention over spatial memory for indoor UAV exploration.

Learned autonomous exploration · 2025 to present · Draft results · KTH benchmark + preliminary aerial simulation

System My contribution Evidence All projects ↗
Status

Draft results from ATLAS_v1. KTH evaluates exploration per unit of motion, excluding planning time and the aerial camera, shield and flight controller. Aerial baseline comparisons, ablations and decision-cost measurements are pending; current-policy hardware validation is not reported.

01 — The system

What it does

ATLAS chooses where an indoor UAV should explore next. It retains observed geometry in a metric map and place graph, keeps a separate token for each current candidate, and reads the remaining places through a fixed bank of learned queries. Graph topology and full-graph route lengths inform goal selection. In aerial execution, each motion prefix is checked against observed free space and a reactive depth shield can stop the drone.

Engineering ownership

My contribution

  • Developed ATLAS for goal selection with topology-aware attention over persistent spatial memory.
  • Trained the compact policy through distillation and reinforcement learning, and evaluated it on KTH floor plans and preliminary aerial simulation scenes.

02 — Evidence

Results in the new draft

Average observable-floor coverage across all 14 KTH floors is 80.36% for ATLAS, compared with 38.74% for CogniPlan and 42.33% for ARiADNE. The shared setting uses a 0.30 m body with 0.05 m padding per face, 8 m sensing and an 1800 s motion budget. Four starts are averaged within each floor, then the 14 floors are weighted equally. This aggregate is not the result of a single video run. Planning time is excluded.

The area under the observable-coverage curve is 0.719 for ATLAS, 0.377 for CogniPlan and 0.413 for ARiADNE. ATLAS is ahead on this measure on all 14 floors, but complete exploration remains uncommon: only 2 of 56 ATLAS episodes reach 99% observable coverage.

Preliminary aerial simulation reports mean newly observed areas of 1714 m² in the school and 1999 m² in the research wing, each from two scored starts using the full 1800 s budget. No collision was recorded in the reported preliminary runs. These are development scenes, some starts remain unfinished, and no aerial baseline comparison is yet available.

Source: ATLAS_v1 draft, Section VI, Tables III–IV and Figure 8. These draft findings do not establish real-flight performance or end-to-end planning speed.

How ATLAS chooses its next goal

Persistent memory, compact readout

Every current candidate keeps its own token. A bank of 64 learned queries reads the other remembered places. For a fixed candidate count, attention cost grows linearly with remembered places; candidate attention remains quadratic in the number of candidates. This is an architectural cost statement, not a measured end-to-end speed result.

Topology-aware candidate attention

Graph adjacency, shared free-space components and distance bias attention between candidates. Route descriptors computed on the full graph retain information about paths through remembered places. Local and global map tokens are fused with the graph representation and recurrent state.

Forecast the whole goal

The network predicts duration, newly observed area and probability of reaching each goal. These forecasts inform the actor. When replanning keeps the same goal, observed segments are joined into one target; abandoned unresolved goals receive no forecast target.

Distillation and reinforcement learning

The compact policy is distilled from an earlier dense-attention policy and then fine-tuned with PPO using duration-aware advantages. The evaluated policy uses collected trajectories only. The auxiliary graph model was fitted with the encoder frozen; imagined actor–critic updates were not enabled.

Independent motion checks

Aerial motion prefixes, including turning and stopping envelopes, must stay within observed free space. A separate 5 cm local grid supports swept-body checks, and a reactive depth shield guards execution between checks. These aerial safety components are outside the KTH floor-plan benchmark.

Design commitments

Retain spatial memory. Learn goal selection. Check motion geometrically.

  • Keep the full measured map and place graph; compress the policy readout without pruning remembered places.
  • Bias candidate attention with graph adjacency, shared free-space components and distance.
  • Train whole-goal forecasts across replanning; exclude abandoned, unresolved goals from forecast targets.
  • Keep learned predictions outside the measured map and geometric safety checks.

What is being tested

The remaining experiments test aerial performance against FALCON, EDEN, CogniPlan and ARiADNE, isolate the contribution of each policy component, and measure complete decision cost as the map grows.
