The arc

From pixels to a map.

Five stages of one pipeline, each shown with real output from the stage that produces it. The frames come from different scenes: the public benchmarks the perception network is measured on, and the building the mapping system was flown through.

  1. 01

    One image in

    Everything downstream starts from a single RGB frame. No laser, no depth camera, no stereo rig — one passive camera is all the system reads.

  2. 02

    Metric depth, learned

    One network predicts metric depth from that single frame. Scale comes from the network, not from a second camera.

  3. 03

    Semantics, same pass

    The same forward pass labels the pixels. Depth, semantics, surface normals and edges are predicted together so the tasks can share what each one learns.

  4. 04

    Where the camera went

    Learned depth backs the visual-inertial estimator, so the trajectory keeps its scale. Recognising a place already visited pulls the estimate back onto itself before the map is committed.

  5. 05

    A structure, not a cloud

    The map that comes out is hierarchical: a mesh carrying semantics, then objects, places, rooms and the building above them. That is the thing a robot can plan over.

Those predictions drive the mapping. Mean mesh error against LiDAR ground truth on the ITC second floor is 0.08 m for M2H-MX-L at 640 × 480 on an RTX 4080 Super, and 0.22 m from the 256 × 192 operating point that runs onboard.

03 — On the robotMono-Hydra++

A camera, an IMU, a map of the building.

My PhD at the University of Twente focuses on indoor mapping for small drones. Mono-Hydra++ builds a layered 3D map from a single camera and an IMU, with perception, odometry and mapping running on the drone’s Jetson.

Visual-inertial estimation

Keeping track through a stairwell

Depth-backed odometry running on the Jetson, tested while carrying the drone down and up the stairs. Zero-velocity updates stabilise the estimate when it stops.

04 — IndustryExpert Hub Robotics · Dubai · 2020–2022

Robotics beyond the lab

Before my PhD, I was the founding robotics engineer at Expert Hub Robotics in Dubai. I built the ROS navigation stack and led deployments of more than 30 humanoid robots across Expo 2020, healthcare and reception sites.

I was promoted to lead a team of three within ten months. Alongside navigation, I fine-tuned a thermal face detector and deployed it in a commercial product using a FLIR camera and an Intel NUC.

Earlier, I worked in chip verification at Synopsys, including on-site support at Samsung Electronics. At Twente, I’ve also designed robotics labs and supervised three master’s thesis students.

05 — NextRobot learning In progress

Where should the robot go next?

Mapping is one part of the problem. I’m now working on how a robot chooses its next route through an unfamiliar building, remembers unexplored areas and returns to unfinished work.

This work is in simulation, with no reported evaluation results yet. It uses its own occupancy map and belief graph; connecting it to Mono-Hydra++ is a next step.
