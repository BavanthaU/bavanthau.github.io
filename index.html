00Trajectory

Small aircraft are the constraint that makes the problem interesting. Payload decides endurance, compute is whatever you can carry, and the answer has to arrive before the next frame. So the work is about getting more out of one passive sensor rather than adding hardware: a single network producing every dense output the downstream system needs, at a frame rate a drone can actually fly on.

That work is a PhD at ITC in Enschede, finishing in 2026. Before it I shipped robots rather than papers. I joined a Dubai startup as its first robotics engineer and within a year was leading a small team deploying more than 30 humanoid robots across Expo 2020, hospitals, and reception desks. Debugging a navigation stack in front of a paying customer is a good way to find out which parts of a system are real.

I started at Peradeniya, building a reconnaissance robot that had to map a space nobody had surveyed. That is still the question I work on, now with a learned model and one camera instead of a laser scanner.

The problem

What I work on

A robot inspecting a bridge, counting warehouse stock, or entering a building after an incident needs more than a point cloud. A point cloud says where surfaces are. It does not say that this is a room, that is a doorway, and there is a fire extinguisher on the wall. Planning, search, and spoken instructions all need objects, places, and rooms.

The sensor is one camera and an IMU: passive, light, inexpensive, information-rich, and geometrically ambiguous, because a single image cannot tell you scale. Every hard problem here follows from choosing not to carry a depth sensor. The reason to accept that is payload, which sets what a small drone can do and how long it can fly.

01Platform

Built for the payload limit

The platform

A custom quadrotor with a carbon fibre frame and caged propellers, so it can fly close to walls indoors. Everything the mapping needs is carried onboard.

02Research

Part I · complete

Structuring the seen

Learned depth and semantics feed a visual-inertial estimator, and the map that comes out is layered: mesh, objects, places, rooms, building. Four papers, from showing it was possible to closing the loop back into the estimator.

  1. Beat 01 · mapping

    The drone flies a corridor and the map builds itself

    One loop of the ITC second floor. Nothing is streamed to a workstation: the camera and the IMU go in, and a layered map comes out, mesh first, then objects, then places, then the rooms that contain them. Watch the graph on the left grow while the corridor closes back on itself.

    How the mapping works

  2. Beat 02 · control

    And the estimate is steady enough to fly on

    The same predictions close the loop back into control. Depth-backed visual-inertial odometry runs on the drone's own Jetson, and zero-velocity updates hold the estimate still when the platform is: the label flips between MOVING and ZUPT STATIONARY. An estimate that drifts at a standstill is an estimate nothing can fly on.

    The estimator, in detail

02.1

What one frame gives the map

M2H-MX turns a single RGB frame into metric depth and semantic labels. The mapping backend consumes those two outputs and nothing else.

02.2

What changed over four years

Mean mapping error on the second floor of the ITC building, measured against a LiDAR ground truth. The embedded point in amber is the model running on the drone at reduced resolution, a different trade-off rather than a regression.

03Next

Part II · work in progress

Exploring the unseen In progress

The agent predicts what each candidate route would produce, in the quantities the decision uses: new area per second, whether it opens a room, how the structure changes, whether it fails. Not pixels. An ensemble separates genuine randomness from the model's own ignorance, and the agent abstains when the second is high.

Selected work

  • ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens.

    Mono-Hydra++: Real-Time Monocular Scene Graph Construction with Multi-Task Learning for 3D Indoor Mapping

    A single camera and an IMU build a hierarchical metric-semantic 3D scene graph of an indoor building in real time, and the map is accurate to 0.08 m.

    Under review at ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing

  • ICRA 2026 SRRA Workshop

    M2H-MX: Multi-Task Semantic and Geometric Perception for Real-Time Monocular 3D Scene Graph Construction

    Better per-frame depth and semantics measurably improve the map and the trajectory, not just the benchmark score.

    Accepted, ICRA 2026 SRRA Workshop

  • IROS 2025

    M2H: Multi-Task Learning with Efficient Window-Based Cross-Task Attention for Monocular Spatial Perception

    Four dense prediction tasks share one small model and make each other better, fast enough to sit inside a mapping loop.

    Published, IEEE/RSJ IROS 2025

  • ISPRS Annals 2023

    Mono-Hydra: Real-Time 3D Scene Graph Construction from Monocular Camera Input with IMU

    A monocular camera and an IMU can build a layered 3D scene graph while the robot is still moving, which the field had only done with depth sensors or LiDAR.

    Published, ISPRS Annals 2023
